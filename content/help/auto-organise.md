# How Mise organises your files

A few seconds after a file lands, Mise looks at it and fills in:

- **A description**, one or two sentences.
- **Tags**: 5–15 words people might search for.
- **Colours**: up to five main colours, named after your brand colours when they match.
- **Text in the image**: any words in the picture, so you can search for them.
- **Alt text** for email and the web, if there isn't any yet.
- **A brand check**: on brand or off brand, with the reason, if your brand kit has imagery rules.
- **A product link**, when it's clearly a photo of one of your products.

Mise never identifies people from their faces.

## Your edits always win
Change a description, tags, the brand check or the product link and Mise keeps your version, even if the file is organised again.

## Watching it work
While files are being organised, a strip at the top of **Assets** shows "Organising 12 files… 30 of 42 done", and the grid fills in as tags arrive. When everything is done it shows "All 42 organised and searchable", then disappears. **Details** opens the full picture in Settings → **Plan & usage** → **Library**.

## Files Mise can't see
SVGs and some other formats can't be looked at by the AI, so they're tagged from their file name ("Tagged from its name").

## When a file can't be read
You'll see "{n} couldn’t be read" on the strip, the file in **Review** under **Couldn’t organise**, and the reason on the file's page.
- Press **Try again** (on the strip, in Review, or on the file). Mise tries each file up to three times on its own first.
- "Too large to organise (over 5 MB). Upload a smaller copy." means the file is very large and has no email-ready copy. Upload a smaller version.
- If it still fails, the file may be damaged or an unusual format. Re-export it as JPG or PNG and upload again.

## Redo
On a file's page, **Redo** (next to "Organised by AI") runs it again. Your edits are kept.

## Older files
If some files were added before organising was switched on, Settings → **Plan & usage** → **Library** shows **Organise {n} older files**.

## Searching by meaning
Organised files can be found by describing them, for example "woman outdoors with a blue bag". The Library section shows how many files are searchable by meaning. See [Finding files](search).
